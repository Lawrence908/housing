Has Housing Rolled Over?

Residential construction is the earliest real-economy turn: housing starts have peaked one to two years before most postwar US recessions, which is why Leamer's Jackson Hole paper is titled "Housing IS the Business Cycle". This page keeps the score: starts and permits since 1959, every rollover dated two ways and measured against what followed, prices in both countries, and Canadian starts back to 1948. Nothing here is a forecast.

Home prices, year over year

Percent change from a year earlier. Prices lag volumes: starts peaked in 2005 while national prices kept rising into 2006-07, and starts bottomed in 2009 while prices fell into 2012. Case-Shiller is repeat-sales since 1987; FHFA reaches 1975 on conforming mortgages; Canada's NHPI covers new construction only, so it misses the resale market and understates swings.

Census starts and permits are revised routinely: each monthly release restates the prior two months, and annual benchmark revisions reach further back. So unlike the yield page, where a revision is an event, this log is expected to be busy; that is the data behaving normally. Every refresh is diffed against the one before it, and any change to an already-published observation is recorded. New observations are not revisions and are not listed.

Sources and construction

United States

  • Starts and permits: Census Bureau and HUD via FRED, all SAAR: HOUST and HOUST1F (monthly since January 1959), PERMIT and PERMIT1 (since January 1960).
  • Sales and supply: HSN1F and MSACSR (since 1963), units under construction UNDCONTSA (since 1970, in the payload).
  • Prices: Case-Shiller national, seasonally adjusted, CSUSHPISA (monthly since 1987); FHFA all-transactions USSTHPI (quarterly since 1975). Year-over-year series are computed here; the index levels ship alongside.
  • The Leamer series: residential investment share of GDP, BEA via A011RE1Q156NBEA, quarterly since 1947. The citation is Leamer, "Housing IS the Business Cycle", Jackson Hole 2007.
  • Mortgage rate: Freddie Mac PMMS via MORTGAGE30US, weekly since April 1971.
  • Recession bands: NBER dates via USREC, from the shared dataset, drawn peak month through trough.

Canada

  • Starts, deep: CMHC via Statistics Canada table 34-10-0135-01, vectors v730416 (total) and v730442 (single-detached): actual units per quarter, unadjusted, from 1948 and 1955, still published. The seasonally-adjusted member of that table (v730427) is terminated at 2009 and deliberately unused; the trailing-4-quarter construction here removes seasonality arithmetically instead.
  • Starts, monthly: table 34-10-0158-01, vector v52300157, all areas, SAAR, from 1990.
  • Prices: new housing price index, table 18-10-0205-01, vector v111955442, monthly since 1981. New construction only; Teranet and CREA resale indexes are not keyless-redistributable and are not shown.
  • Recession bands: C.D. Howe Institute Business Cycle Council chronology, from the shared dataset.
  • Every StatCan vector is fetched with its live English title verified against the published table first; a renumbered vector fails loudly rather than charting someone else's numbers.

Plumbing

  • FRED is fetched keyless-first (public CSV), keyed API as fallback; StatCan WDS has no keys at all. A failed fetch carries the stored series forward and says so rather than blanking a chart.
  • Refreshes daily via cron. Releases scatter across the month (starts mid-month, permits later, Case-Shiller the last Tuesday, FHFA quarterly, StatCan on its own calendar), so most runs verify rather than change; the stamp above says when data last moved.
  • Series follow the shared econ-core contract used across yield, jobs, debt and diesel, with a vintages field reserved for as-published (ALFRED) views. Housing is where that will matter most: starts revisions are material, and "what did the rollover look like in real time in 2006" is the honest version of this page's main chart.
